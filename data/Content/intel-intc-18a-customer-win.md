Intel's November has been a story of two very different narratives fighting for control of the stock.

## The rough patch

- **Nov 16:** INTC fell 7.0% to $55.47 after a shareholder class action named CEO **Lip-Bu Tan** and CFO **David Zinsner** as defendants, alleging Intel didn't promptly disclose how falling PC sales and an inventory buildup would hit revenue. The suit covers investors who bought shares between July 23 and October 29, 2026.
- The same week, a filing showed **Nvidia cut its stake in Intel by 25%**.

## The turnaround signal

- **Nov 9:** INTC rose 5.5% to $59.37 as chip stocks broadly rebounded.
- **Nov 18:** Intel signed its **first external customer for advanced 18A chips** — a genuine proof point for the foundry turnaround strategy — and announced an expanded Microsoft Azure collaboration the same day.
- Despite the lawsuit and the pullback, INTC is still **up 48% year-to-date**, powered by surging server demand and real progress on the manufacturing side. Some analysts now see a path back toward **$85** on the strength of the 18A win.

## Why it matters

The lawsuit is a real overhang, but it's a backward-looking dispute about disclosure timing. The 18A customer win is forward-looking and strikes at the core question investors have had about Intel for years: can its foundry business actually compete? One external customer isn't proof, but it's the first real data point.
