## Two kinds of risk

- **Specific (idiosyncratic) risk:** a single company fails, loses a lawsuit or misses earnings. Diversification can almost eliminate this risk.
- **Systematic (market) risk:** recessions, interest-rate shocks, crises. This affects nearly everything and can't be diversified away within stocks. Bonds and cash can soften it.

## Layers of diversification

1. **Across companies:** an index fund with hundreds of holdings.
2. **Across sectors:** technology, healthcare, financials, energy, consumer and more.
3. **Across countries:** a home-country-only portfolio ties your wealth to one economy, where you already earn your salary.
4. **Across asset classes:** stocks, bonds, real estate, cash.

## Beware of hidden concentration

Market-cap-weighted indexes can become concentrated. At times, the largest ten companies have made up more than a third of the S&P 500. Owning an S&P 500 fund, a Nasdaq 100 fund and a "global" fund may mean owning the same mega-cap tech names three times.

## Over-diversification

Holding 15 funds that all track similar indexes adds complexity, not safety. Two to four well-chosen broad funds are usually enough.
