Choosing which fund to buy gets most of the attention. For a diversified investor, the bigger decision is **how much to put in each asset class**.

## Sample allocations

| Profile | Stocks | Bonds | Cash | Typical worst year (historical) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 30% | 60% | 10% | about −10% |
| Balanced | 60% | 35% | 5% | about −20% |
| Growth | 80% | 20% | 0% | about −30% |
| Aggressive | 100% | 0% | 0% | −40% or worse |

These figures are rough historical ranges, not forecasts.

## Three questions to set your mix

1. **Time horizon:** When will you need the money? Longer horizons can hold more stocks.
2. **Risk tolerance:** If your portfolio fell 30%, would you hold, buy more, or sell? Be honest.
3. **Risk capacity:** Could you financially absorb the loss? A secure income and a large emergency fund increase capacity.

## Age-based rules of thumb

An old rule says to hold "100 minus your age" in stocks. With people living longer, many planners now use 110 or 120 minus age. Israeli pension and study funds offer age-based tracks (*maslul taluy gil*) that shift from stocks to bonds automatically.

## Diversify within each asset class

Within stocks, spread across countries, sectors and company sizes. See [Diversification](#/learn/diversification).
