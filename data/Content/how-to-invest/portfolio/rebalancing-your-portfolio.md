## Why portfolios drift

Suppose you start at 60% stocks and 40% bonds. After a strong year for stocks you might be at 70/30, taking more risk than you planned just before a potential downturn.

## Methods

| Method | How it works | Pros / cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Calendar** | Rebalance on a fixed date, e.g. every January | Simple and predictable; may trade when unnecessary |
| **Threshold** | Rebalance when an asset drifts more than ±5 points | Responds to big moves; needs monitoring |
| **Cash-flow** | Direct new money (or withdrawals) to the underweight asset | Low cost and tax-friendly; slower in large portfolios |

## Taxes and costs

In a taxable account, selling winners realizes capital gains. Where possible:

- Rebalance inside tax-advantaged accounts first.
- Use new contributions and dividends to buy the underweight asset.
- Avoid rebalancing too often. Once or twice a year is plenty for most people.

## The behavioral benefit

Rebalancing forces you to sell high and buy low, the opposite of what fear and greed suggest. It is a built-in discipline.
