## The seesaw

Say you own a bond paying a 3% coupon. Then interest rates rise and new bonds pay 5%. No one will pay full price for your 3% bond when they can get 5% elsewhere, so its market price falls until its yield matches the new rate. The opposite happens when rates fall.

## Duration: measuring sensitivity

**Duration** (in years) approximates how much a bond's price moves when rates change by one percentage point.

| Duration | Approx. price change if rates rise 1% |
|---|---|
| 2 years | −2% |
| 7 years | −7% |
| 17 years | −17% |

Long-term government bond funds can therefore fall sharply in a rising-rate environment, as they did in 2022.

## Choosing a duration

- **Short duration (1–3 years):** small price swings, a good place for money you will need soon.
- **Intermediate (4–7 years):** a common core holding.
- **Long (10+ years):** the biggest gains when rates fall and the biggest losses when they rise.

## Holding to maturity

If you hold an individual high-quality bond to maturity, interim price swings don't change what you receive, barring default. Bond funds never mature, but over time they reinvest at the new, higher yields, which gradually offsets the earlier price losses.
