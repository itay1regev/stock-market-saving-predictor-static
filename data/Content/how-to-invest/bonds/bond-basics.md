When you buy a bond, you lend money to the issuer. In return you receive regular interest payments, called the **coupon**, and get your **principal** (face value) back on the **maturity date**.

## Key terms

- **Face value:** the amount repaid at maturity, often 1,000 or 100.
- **Coupon rate:** the annual interest as a percentage of face value.
- **Maturity:** when the loan ends. This can be months or 30+ years.
- **Yield to maturity (YTM):** your total annual return if you buy at today's price and hold to maturity.

## Types of bonds

| Type | Issuer | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Government | US Treasury, Israeli *shahar* and *galil* | Lowest credit risk in their home currency |
| Inflation-linked | US TIPS, Israeli CPI-linked *galil* | Principal adjusts with inflation |
| Corporate investment grade | Large, financially strong companies | Higher yield than government bonds |
| High-yield ("junk") | Weaker companies | Higher yield, higher default risk |

## Risks

- **Interest-rate risk:** bond prices fall when rates rise. See [Interest Rates and Bond Prices](#/learn/interest-rates-and-bond-prices).
- **Credit risk:** the issuer might fail to pay.
- **Inflation risk:** fixed coupons lose purchasing power when inflation is high.
