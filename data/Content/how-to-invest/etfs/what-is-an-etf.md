An exchange-traded fund (ETF) is a fund that holds a basket of investments, such as stocks, bonds or commodities, and whose shares trade on a stock exchange like an ordinary share.

## How ETFs work

When you buy one share of an S&P 500 ETF, you indirectly own a small slice of all 500 companies in the index, weighted by market value. Specialist firms called *authorized participants* create and redeem ETF shares in large blocks. This keeps the ETF's price close to the value of its holdings.

## Why investors like ETFs

- **Instant diversification** in a single purchase
- **Low costs:** broad index ETFs can charge under 0.1% a year
- **Transparency:** most ETFs publish their holdings daily
- **Flexibility:** buy and sell any time the market is open

## Types of ETFs

| Type | Example exposure |
|---|---|
| Broad equity | S&P 500, MSCI World, TA-125 |
| Sector | Technology, healthcare, banks |
| Bond | Government, corporate, inflation-linked |
| Thematic | AI, clean energy, cybersecurity |
| Leveraged / inverse | 2× or −1× daily index return: trading tools, not long-term holdings |

## How to compare ETFs that track the same index

1. **Expense ratio:** the annual fee.
2. **Tracking difference:** how closely returns have matched the index after costs.
3. **Assets under management and trading volume:** larger funds usually trade at tighter bid–ask spreads.
4. **Domicile and distribution policy:** this affects withholding tax and whether dividends are paid out or reinvested.
