Investing means putting money into assets, such as shares of companies, bonds or funds, that you expect to grow in value or pay you income over time. Unlike a savings account, investments can lose value in the short run. The trade-off is a higher expected return over the long run.

## Step 1: Get your foundation in place

Before you buy a single share, make sure a market drop can't force you to sell at the worst moment.

- **Emergency fund.** Keep three to six months of essential expenses in cash or a deposit account.
- **High-interest debt.** Paying off a credit card that charges 18% a year is a guaranteed 18% "return". No investment reliably beats that.
- **Insurance.** Health, disability and life insurance protect the plan you are about to build.

## Step 2: Define your goals and time horizon

Write down what you are investing for and when you will need the money.

| Goal | Time horizon | Typical approach |
|---|---|---|
| Home down payment | 1–4 years | Mostly cash and short-term bonds |
| Child's education | 5–15 years | Balanced mix of stocks and bonds |
| Retirement | 15+ years | Mostly stocks, shifting to bonds later |

The longer your horizon, the more short-term volatility you can afford to ride out.

## Step 3: Choose the right account

Where you hold an investment can matter as much as what you buy. Tax-advantaged accounts, such as an Israeli *keren hishtalmut* or a US 401(k) or IRA, let your money compound with little or no tax drag. Read our guides to [Israeli long-term savings](#/learn/israeli-long-term-savings) and [US retirement accounts](#/learn/us-retirement-accounts). For everything else, open a regular brokerage account with a low-cost, well-regulated broker.

## Step 4: Pick a simple investment

Most beginners do best with one or two broad, low-cost index funds, for example a global stock fund that tracks the MSCI World or a US fund that tracks the S&P 500. You can compare such funds on our [ETF pages](#/etf/sp500). If you want less volatility, add a bond fund.

## Step 5: Automate and stay the course

Set up a fixed monthly transfer and purchase. This approach, called **dollar-cost averaging**, removes the temptation to time the market. Then check in once or twice a year, rebalance if your mix has drifted, and resist reacting to headlines.
