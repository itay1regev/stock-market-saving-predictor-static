There is no minimum amount required to become an investor. What matters most is consistency and keeping costs low.

## Small amounts are fine

Many brokers now offer **fractional shares**, so you can buy a slice of a fund or stock for a small amount instead of paying the full share price. Pension and study funds in Israel accept monthly deposits of any size.

## Mind the costs

On small amounts, fixed fees hurt. A 5 commission on a 100 purchase is a 5% cost before the investment has moved. To keep costs down:

- Choose a broker with low or zero commissions on the funds you plan to buy.
- Invest monthly or quarterly rather than many tiny purchases.
- Prefer funds with low annual expense ratios.

## A practical starting plan

1. Decide on a monthly amount you can sustain, even if it is small.
2. Pick one broad index fund.
3. Automate the transfer and purchase.
4. Increase the amount whenever your income rises.

The best amount to start with is the one you can keep investing every month.
