People often use "saving" and "investing" to mean the same thing. They are different tools that do different jobs.

## Saving: safety and access

Saving means setting aside money in a bank deposit, savings account or money-market fund. Your balance does not fall and you can usually withdraw quickly. The cost of that safety is a low return, often at or below the rate of inflation, so the money's purchasing power can slowly erode.

## Investing: growth with volatility

Investing means buying assets such as stocks, bonds or real estate. Their value goes up and down, sometimes sharply. Over long periods, broad stock markets have delivered returns well above inflation, but there have been years when they fell 30% or more.

## Which should you use?

| | Saving | Investing |
|---|---|---|
| Main goal | Preserve capital | Grow capital |
| Risk of loss | Very low | Moderate to high |
| Best for | Emergencies, goals under ~3 years | Goals 5+ years away |
| Inflation protection | Weak | Historically strong |

A simple rule: **if you might need the money within a few years, save it. If you won't, consider investing it.** Most households hold both, an emergency fund in savings and a long-term portfolio in investments.
