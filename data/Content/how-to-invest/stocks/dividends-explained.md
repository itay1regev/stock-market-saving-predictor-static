A dividend is a portion of a company's profit paid out to shareholders, usually quarterly in the US and once or twice a year in Israel.

## Key dates

1. **Declaration date:** the board announces the dividend.
2. **Ex-dividend date:** you must own the shares *before* this date to receive the payment. The share price typically drops by about the dividend amount on this day.
3. **Record date:** the company checks its list of shareholders.
4. **Payment date:** the cash arrives in your account.

## Dividend yield

**Yield = Annual dividend per share ÷ Share price**

A 100 stock paying 3 a year yields 3%. A very high yield, say 10% or more, often means the price has fallen because investors expect the dividend to be cut. Check the **payout ratio** (dividends ÷ earnings). A ratio consistently above 80–90% may not be sustainable, except for structures such as REITs that must distribute most of their income.

## Reinvesting

Historically, a large share of the stock market's total return has come from reinvested dividends. Many funds offer *accumulating* share classes that reinvest automatically, which can also be more tax-efficient.

## Taxes

Dividends are usually taxed when they are paid. Israeli residents generally pay 25% on dividends. US stocks also withhold tax at source, typically reduced to 25% for Israeli residents with a W-8BEN on file. Rules change, so check with a tax adviser.
