## 1. Choose a broker

Look for a broker that is regulated in your country, offers access to the exchanges you want (for example TASE, NYSE and Nasdaq), and charges low commissions and custody fees. Check currency-conversion costs if you plan to buy foreign shares.

## 2. Open and fund the account

You will need identification and tax details. Transfer money from your bank account. If you buy US stocks from Israel, you will usually complete a W-8BEN form so that the reduced treaty rate applies to dividend withholding tax.

## 3. Research the company

Before buying, understand:

- What the company sells and how it makes money
- Whether revenue and profits are growing
- How much debt it carries
- How its valuation compares with peers. See [P/E Ratio and Valuation](#/learn/pe-ratio-and-valuation).

## 4. Place your order

| Order type | What it does | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Market** | Buys immediately at the best available price | Large, liquid stocks during market hours |
| **Limit** | Buys only at your price or better | Less liquid stocks, or when you want price control |
| **Stop-loss** | Sells if the price falls to a set level | To cap the loss on a position |

Beginners are usually safer with **limit orders**, which prevent paying far more than expected in a fast market.

## 5. Size your position sensibly

A common guideline is to keep any single stock below 5% of your total portfolio. Hold the core of your money in diversified funds.
