## What the P/E ratio measures

**P/E = Share price ÷ Earnings per share (EPS)**

A stock at 100 with EPS of 5 has a P/E of 20. Investors are paying 20 for every 1 of annual profit.

- **Trailing P/E** uses the past 12 months of earnings.
- **Forward P/E** uses analysts' estimates for the next 12 months.

## Reading the number

A high P/E usually means investors expect fast growth. A low P/E can mean a bargain, or a business in decline. Context matters:

- Software companies often trade at higher P/Es than banks or utilities.
- When interest rates are low, the whole market's P/E tends to rise.

## Other valuation tools

| Ratio | Formula | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| **PEG** | P/E ÷ expected growth rate | Comparing fast growers |
| **P/B** | Price ÷ book value per share | Banks, insurers, real estate |
| **P/S** | Market cap ÷ revenue | Companies not yet profitable |
| **EV/EBITDA** | Enterprise value ÷ EBITDA | Comparing firms with different debt levels |
| **Dividend yield** | Annual dividend ÷ price | Income-focused investing |

## Pitfalls

Earnings can be distorted by one-off gains, write-downs or accounting choices. Cyclical companies often look cheapest at the top of the cycle, when profits peak.
