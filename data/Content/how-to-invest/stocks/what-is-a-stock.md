When you buy a share of stock, you become a part-owner of a company. You are entitled to a small piece of its profits and usually a vote on certain decisions.

## How stocks make money for you

1. **Capital gains:** the share price rises above what you paid.
2. **Dividends:** the company pays part of its profits to shareholders in cash. See [Dividends Explained](#/learn/dividends-explained).

## Why stock prices move

A share price reflects what buyers and sellers think the company's future cash flows are worth today. Prices move when that view changes, for example after earnings reports, changes in interest rates, new products, or a shift in economic outlook. Over months, emotion can push prices far from fundamentals. Over decades, prices tend to follow the growth in profits.

## Common types of stock

- **Common vs. preferred:** common shares carry voting rights. Preferred shares usually pay fixed dividends and rank ahead of common shares if the company is liquidated.
- **Growth vs. value:** growth companies reinvest profits to expand quickly. Value stocks trade cheaply relative to their earnings or assets.
- **Large-cap vs. small-cap:** this describes the company's total market value. Large caps tend to be more stable.

## Single stocks vs. funds

Owning a single company is risky. Even great businesses can lose most of their value. Most investors hold stocks through a diversified fund.
