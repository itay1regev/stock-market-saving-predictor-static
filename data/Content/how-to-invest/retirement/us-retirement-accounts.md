## 401(k) and similar employer plans

Offered through your job, with contributions taken directly from your paycheck. Many employers **match** part of your contribution, for example 50% of what you put in up to 6% of salary. Investment choices are limited to the plan's menu, which usually includes target-date and index funds.

## IRA (Individual Retirement Account)

Opened at any broker and available if you have earned income. Annual contribution limits are lower than for a 401(k), but you can invest in almost any ETF or stock.

## Traditional vs. Roth

| | Traditional | Roth |
|---|---|---|
| Contributions | Pre-tax (deductible) | After-tax |
| Growth | Tax-deferred | Tax-free |
| Withdrawals in retirement | Taxed as income | Tax-free (if rules met) |
| Best if… | You expect a lower tax rate in retirement | You expect a higher tax rate later |

## A common funding order

1. Contribute enough to your 401(k) to get the **full employer match**.
2. Fund a **Roth or traditional IRA** up to the limit.
3. Go back and raise **401(k) contributions** toward the maximum.
4. Invest anything extra in a **taxable brokerage account**.

## Early withdrawals

Withdrawing before age 59½ generally triggers income tax plus a 10% penalty, with some exceptions. Treat these accounts as long-term money.

*Contribution limits change each year. Check current IRS figures.*
