Israel has three main long-term savings vehicles. They are often confused, but each has a distinct role.

## Pension fund (קרן פנסיה)

- **Mandatory** for employees, with contributions from both employee and employer.
- Provides a monthly annuity in retirement, plus **disability and survivors' insurance** while you are working.
- Charges two kinds of management fee: on deposits and on accumulated balance. Both are negotiable.

## Provident fund (קופת גמל)

- A long-term savings account, often used for amounts above the pension ceiling, or by the self-employed.
- **Kupat gemel le'hashka'a** (investment provident fund) allows withdrawals at any time, taxed on real gains. If held until retirement age it can be converted to an annuity with tax benefits.
- There is no insurance component, so all the money is invested.

## Study fund (קרן השתלמות)

- Employee and employer contribute, typically 2.5% and 7.5% of salary, up to a ceiling.
- **Liquid after six years** (or three years for certain purposes), with gains **exempt from capital-gains tax** within the ceiling.
- Often called the best tax-advantaged savings vehicle available to Israeli employees.

## Choosing a track and a provider

1. **Investment track:** general, equity, S&P 500-linked, halachic, or age-based. A younger saver with a long horizon can usually hold a higher equity share.
2. **Fees:** compare deposit and balance fees, which are negotiable.
3. **Performance:** look at long-term (five-year) risk-adjusted returns rather than last year's winner.
