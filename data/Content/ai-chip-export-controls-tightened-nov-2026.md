## What happened

- On Nov 16, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued a rule requiring licenses for AI chip or server exports to data centers anywhere in the world if the compute could reach China or other restricted countries, and added new licensing requirements for Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil.
- Reporting explicitly linked the tightened rule to the Nvidia espionage scandal that preceded Jensen Huang's temporary resignation.
- AMD fell roughly 4-5% on Nov 16 on the news; Nvidia separately warned chip buyers of legal risk tied to China AI access.
- Separately, it was reported on Nov 19 that the US had signed off on sales of nearly 900,000 AI chips to China over the prior six months -- a reminder the policy picture is not a simple one-way clampdown.

## Why it matters

Export-control policy is now a first-order risk factor for the entire AI chip supply chain -- Nvidia, TSMC, and AMD all move on these headlines independent of their own earnings. Worth tracking as its own story line, separate from any single company's quarter.
