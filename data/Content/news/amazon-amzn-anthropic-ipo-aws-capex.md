Amazon's stock chart in November tells a story of short-term stumbles layered on top of an enormous, deliberate AI bet.

## The stumbles

- **Nov 5:** AMZN fell 2.2% as a broad tech selloff erased early gains from its new Uber Eats partnership for Prime members.
- **Nov 11:** AMZN fell 1.7% on Anthropic's amended IPO filing — notable because Amazon holds a **14.6% stake** in Anthropic.
- **Nov 16:** AMZN fell 3.3% after the loss of 40 **Amazon Leo** satellites in a failed rocket launch, compounded by a new US rule tightening access to advanced AI chips.
- **Nov 19:** AMZN fell 3.9% following Anthropic's actual Nasdaq debut — the stake that was supposed to be a tailwind instead coincided with a down day for AMZN itself.

## The bigger bet underneath

- CEO **Andy Jassy** is pushing roughly **$220 billion** in 2026 capex, overwhelmingly directed at data centers, servers, and networking to support AWS and Amazon Bedrock.
- Despite the choppy month, analysts have been raising targets: **UBS to $352**, **Freedom Broker from $255 to $330**, and **Oppenheimer to $310** after the Anthropic IPO.
- Amazon is also reportedly eyeing a **$7.5B AI startup deal** as competition with Nvidia's ecosystem heats up.

## Why it matters

None of November's drops were about Amazon's core retail or cloud business underperforming — they were about AI-adjacent headline risk (a satellite failure, chip export rules, a related company's IPO). Meanwhile, the capex number and the analyst target hikes suggest the people who cover this stock closely aren't rattled by the daily noise. Worth separating the headline-driven dips from the underlying thesis.
