The inflation measure the Federal Reserve watches most closely came in hotter than policymakers would like.

## By the numbers

| Measure | Year over year (October) |
|---|---|
| Headline PCE | **3.8%** |
| Core PCE (excluding food and energy) | **3.3%** |
| Fed target | 2.0% |

## What it means

The **personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index** tracks what households actually spend money on and is the Fed's official inflation benchmark. With both headline and core inflation well above target, the data strengthens the case for the rate hike markets expect on Dec. 9.

## Why it matters for your money

- **Cash and short-term bonds:** higher rates generally mean better yields on deposits and money-market funds.
- **Longer-term bonds:** rising rate expectations push their prices down. See [Why Bond Prices Fall When Interest Rates Rise](#/learn/interest-rates-and-bond-prices).
- **Stocks:** higher rates raise the discount rate on future profits, which weighs hardest on high-valuation growth stocks.
