Investors who hoped falling tech stocks would soften the Federal Reserve's stance are being disappointed. Traders still see a rate hike as close to certain at the Fed's next meeting.

## What happened

- CME Group's FedWatch data put the probability of a 25-basis-point hike at the **Dec. 9 FOMC meeting** at roughly **98%** this week. Prediction market Polymarket showed somewhat lower odds, around 91%, down from about 98% in mid-November.
- Minutes from the October FOMC meeting showed a **strong consensus among officials in favor of raising rates in December**.
- The Fed's most recent move was a 25 bp hike in September, its first increase since 2023.
- Economist Claudia Sahm said the stock market would need to fall much further before it would change the Fed's course.

## Why it matters

A second hike would push borrowing costs higher just as growth worries build around the AI investment boom. Schwab noted that rate-hike expectations eased somewhat during the week, as some investors began to worry that a slowdown in AI spending could weigh on growth. For bond investors, higher policy rates usually mean pressure on longer-duration bonds. For savers, they mean better yields on cash.

## What to watch

The Dec. 9 decision and the Fed's updated projections, or "dot plot," for 2027. Any signal that this hike is the last one could matter as much as the hike itself.
