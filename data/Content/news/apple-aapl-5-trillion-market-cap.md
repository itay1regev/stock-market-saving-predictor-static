Apple just hit a number that sounds almost unreal: a $5 trillion market capitalization, backed by its first-ever close above $350 a share.

## The milestone

- Apple's 12-month return through Nov 12 was **29.4%**, more than double the S&P 500's 12.2% over the same stretch.
- **Nov 9:** AAPL rose 2.43% to a record close of $350.69.
- **Nov 4:** AAPL rose 5.3% to $349.51 as Big Tech rallied broadly after the midterm elections.
- The stock now trades **above** the average analyst price target of $340.77 (from 45 analysts), who collectively rate it a consensus Buy.

## The asterisk

- **Nov 2 earnings:** Revenue hit **$478.8B, up 15%** year over year — a strong headline number.
- But guidance spooked some investors: management guided December-quarter gross margin to **45–46%**, down from 47.9% in the September quarter. AAPL fell 1.9% to $331.90 on Nov 3 as a result.
- Executives pointed to two specific causes: **rising memory costs** and the ramp-up spend on the **Siri AI overhaul**.

## Why it matters

The $5T headline is the story everyone will share. The margin guidance is the story worth actually tracking — Apple is explicitly trading some near-term profitability for an AI bet on Siri, at the same time memory input costs are rising industry-wide (the same dynamic hitting plenty of other hardware makers right now). Revenue growth is real; the margin trade-off is the thing to watch into next quarter's print.
