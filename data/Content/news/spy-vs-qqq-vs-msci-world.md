"Just buy an index fund" is common advice — but which index? SPY, QQQ, and MSCI World all get recommended as simple, diversified core holdings, yet they're meaningfully different products.

## SPY: the S&P 500

SPY tracks the S&P 500, roughly the 500 largest publicly traded U.S. companies, weighted by market capitalization. It's broad within the U.S. market — spanning technology, healthcare, financials, energy, and more — but it is entirely U.S.-based.

Because it's weighted by market cap, SPY's performance is still meaningfully influenced by its largest holdings, which in recent years have skewed heavily toward a handful of large technology companies. It's diversified across sectors, but concentrated at the top.

## QQQ: the Nasdaq 100

QQQ tracks the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq. In practice, this means a much heavier tilt toward technology and growth companies than SPY. QQQ has historically delivered strong returns during tech-driven bull markets, but that same concentration means it can fall harder during tech-sector downturns.

QQQ is not really a "total market" fund — it's a growth-and-tech-tilted fund that happens to be popular enough to be treated like one. Worth knowing before assuming it behaves like a generic index.

## MSCI World: true global diversification

MSCI World (tracked by ETFs like URTH) holds large and mid-cap companies across roughly two dozen developed markets, including the U.S., Japan, the U.K., and much of Europe. This gives it geographic diversification that SPY and QQQ don't have — if the U.S. market underperforms for a stretch, other developed markets in the index may not.

The tradeoff is that MSCI World is still heavily weighted toward the U.S. (U.S. companies make up a large share of global market capitalization), so the diversification benefit is real but partial, not a complete hedge against U.S.-specific downturns.

## How to think about the choice

- **Want simple, broad U.S. exposure?** SPY is the standard choice, and its long history makes it easy to backtest against decades of data.
- **Want to lean into growth and technology, understanding the extra volatility that comes with it?** QQQ fits, but should be sized as a satellite holding for many investors rather than a sole core position.
- **Want geographic diversification beyond the U.S.?** MSCI World spreads exposure across developed markets, at the cost of somewhat lower historical returns compared to SPY over the last decade.

None of these is objectively "best" — they answer different questions about how much concentration and geography you're comfortable with. Running the same contribution schedule through a backtest against each one is a fast way to see how differently they've actually behaved.
