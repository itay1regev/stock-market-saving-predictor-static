If you want proof that TSMC's stock is a direct read-through on Nvidia, November 2026 is the textbook case.

## A month that tracked Nvidia almost tick for tick

- **Nov 12:** TSM fell 1.9% to $405.49 on the news of Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia.
- **Nov 17:** TSM dropped 3.7% to $359.69 after media tied the move to a new US rule tightening checks on chip foundries.
- **Nov 20:** TSM fell another 9.5% to $318.71.
- **Nov 23:** The moment Nvidia's board reinstated Huang as CEO, TSM rocketed **14.1%** to close at $363.60 — its best day in years, directly tied to its role as Nvidia's exclusive manufacturing partner.
- Earlier in the month, TSM had jumped 6.3% on an unconfirmed report that it's drawing up plans to build stacked DRAM for Nvidia — nothing signed yet, but the market treated it as a credible signal.

## The bigger picture

Even after the big bounce, TSM still sits roughly **25% below its 52-week high**, despite outperforming the Nasdaq by 7.6 percentage points over the stretch. That gap is the clearest sign of how much "Nvidia risk" is currently priced into TSMC shares — for better and for worse.

## Why it matters

TSMC's fundamentals (foundry capacity, node leadership, customer backlog) move slowly. Its stock price, this month, moved entirely on headlines about one customer's leadership and export-control rules. That's the concentration risk every TSMC investor is implicitly taking on.
