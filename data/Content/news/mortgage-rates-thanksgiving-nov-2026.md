Homebuyers got a small break this week, though borrowing remains expensive by recent standards.

## The latest numbers

- The average **30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to about 7.21%** in Freddie Mac's survey released Wednesday, Nov. 25, down from roughly 7.28% the prior week.
- The Mortgage Bankers Association said in mid-November that borrowing costs had fallen almost 30 basis points from a nearly two-year high of about **7.5%** reached in October.
- A year ago, the 30-year rate was close to **6.2%**. Today's buyers pay about a full percentage point more.
- Daily trackers showed the average 30-year refinance rate near **7.0%**.

## What a 1% difference costs

On a 400,000 loan, a 30-year mortgage at 7.2% costs roughly 2,715 a month in principal and interest, against about 2,450 at 6.2%. That is around 265 more each month, or more than 95,000 over the life of the loan.

## What to do now

1. **Shop several lenders.** Quotes for the same borrower can differ meaningfully.
2. **Compare points against rate.** Paying points lowers the rate but only pays off if you keep the loan long enough.
3. **Don't stretch the budget** in the hope of refinancing later. Rates may not fall as quickly as expected, especially with the Fed expected to raise rates in December.
