Bitcoin is heading for one of its worst months in years, and the selling has spread through the crypto market.

## What happened

- Bitcoin fell to a **two-year low of roughly $55,600 around Nov. 21**, then rebounded to about **$60,000–$61,500**.
- Estimates of the month-to-date decline range from about **16% to 23%**, depending on the source and starting point.
- The price is now close to **50% below its October 2025 peak** of about $126,000.
- **Spot bitcoin ETFs saw heavy outflows.** Reported November totals range from about $2.9 billion to more than $5 billion, and BlackRock's IBIT reportedly posted its largest single-day outflow.
- More than **$2 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated** in a single 24-hour window during a mid-November weekend.

## Why now?

Analysts point to the same forces hitting tech stocks: expectations of another Fed rate hike, hot inflation data and a sell-off in AI and chip names. These have pushed investors away from speculative, high-volatility assets.

## The takeaway for investors

Crypto has fallen 50% or more from its highs several times before. If you hold it, size the position so that a drawdown like this doesn't derail your financial plan. A common guideline is to keep speculative assets to a small single-digit percentage of a portfolio.
